# Premise Challenge and Alternatives

Challenge the opportunity before settling on implementation. Ask each unresolved question individually and **STOP** after it:

- Is the stated problem evidenced in the declared inputs, or is only a proposed solution present?
- What happens if nobody builds anything? What counterevidence suggests that the problem may still matter?
- Does existing code, a current workflow or an available substitute already address the need?
- Which buyer, user and payer are distinct, and what supplied input supports each relationship?
- What distribution path is assumed, who controls it, and which of those facts are actually supplied?

Do not assume that a buyer exists, that a user can authorize a purchase, or that a substitute is inferior. Mark unsupported answers as assumptions or unknowns.

## Mandatory alternatives

Before concluding, present at least two distinct build alternatives and a third explicit **Stop / do nothing** option. Alternatives may be labeled **Minimal**, **Ideal**, and (when useful) **Lateral**. For every option include:

- the user outcome and smallest scope;
- **Effort**, **Risk**, and **Reuses** (existing code, workflows or declared capabilities; say “none identified” only after checking supplied inputs);
- the key assumption and the evidence that could discriminate it.

Do not rank alternatives from fabricated market evidence. Explain any provisional preference and its basis, then **STOP** and wait for the user's response before choosing or changing scope. If recommending Stop, record the rationale and counterevidence in the brief. If no evidence discriminates the alternatives, say so and make the next discriminating action explicit.
